## 6. Are They Reproducible And Trustworthy?

They are partially reproducible as local development tests, but not trustworthy as final thesis evaluation evidence.

Reproducibility limitations:

- No artifact embeds the exact command used to generate it.
- No artifact embeds a run timestamp, git commit hash, branch, dirty-worktree status, environment variables, service URLs, database state, model versions, service health, or Docker/container state.
- Four generator scripts hard-code port `8000`, while the verified orchestrator default is `8004`.
- `routeBenchmark.js` was last modified on 2026-05-16, after `route_accuracy_report.json` was last written on 2026-05-02. Therefore, the current generator script cannot be proven to be identical to the script that produced the committed route report.
- The tests depend on live services, mutable model behavior, local databases, Qdrant/Neo4j contents, and current route logic.
- Several outputs are failure states, not successful benchmark results.

Trustworthiness limitations from artifact contents:

- `route_accuracy_report.json` reports 35 total queries, 11.43% route accuracy, 88.57% misroutes, 100.00% fallback frequency, and all non-fallback classes routed to `LLM_FALLBACK`.
- `latency_report.json` reports 35 total queries and 35 network failures, with no route latency results.
- `frontend_contract_report.json` reports 0.00% contract success, 100.00% malformed payload rate, and 100.00% frontend break risk.
- `failure_simulation_report.json` reports 0.00% graceful degradation and 100.00% catastrophic failure.
- `hallucination_report.json` reports 8.33% hallucination rate and marks the `<5%` hallucination target as FAIL.

These artifacts may be useful as evidence that a local benchmark attempt was made and that it exposed failures. They are not reliable evidence of final system performance, final route accuracy, final latency, final reliability, or final frontend contract stability.
